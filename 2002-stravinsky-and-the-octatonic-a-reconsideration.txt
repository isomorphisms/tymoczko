Stravinsky and the Octatonic: A Reconsideration
Dmitri Tymoczko
Music Theory Spectrum 24(1), 2002, pp. 68-102
DOI 10.1525/mts.2002.24.1.68

Why it matters

This is Tymoczko's first major published theory article in the lineage that leads toward A Geometry of Music. It is not yet the Möbius-strip paper. Its importance is that it refuses to explain Stravinsky with one privileged collection and instead separates several interacting scalar techniques.

Tymoczko argues that Stravinsky's sound cannot simply be identified with octatonicism. He emphasizes modal uses of non-diatonic minor scales and the superimposition of material drawn from different scalar collections.

Connection to the later geometry

The later book treats scale, macroharmony, centricity, harmony, and voice leading as related but non-identical structures. This 2002 paper is an early example of the same methodological habit: do not collapse several musical phenomena into a single label.

For a computational representation, this argues against storing only a chord name or only a pitch-class set. Keep the active scale or collection, the sounding pitches, and the voice-leading relation as separable information.
