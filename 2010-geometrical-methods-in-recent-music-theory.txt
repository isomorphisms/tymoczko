Geometrical Methods in Recent Music Theory
Dmitri Tymoczko
Music Theory Online 16(1), 2010
DOI 10.30535/mto.16.1.7

What it is

This commentary clarifies how continuous voice-leading spaces relate to discrete graphs used elsewhere in recent music theory. Tymoczko emphasizes that discrete voice-leading graphs can be embedded in the broader continuous spaces developed with Clifton Callender and Ian Quinn.

Why it matters for the repository

A discrete representation and a continuous representation do not have to compete.

Twelve equal-tempered pitch classes, a particular chord graph, or a finite lattice can be treated as a selected discrete subset of a continuous ambient pitch/chord space. This is a useful computational architecture:

- preserve continuous pitch values underneath;
- derive discrete equal-tempered or scale-specific points when needed;
- derive graph edges from a chosen voice-leading rule;
- keep the rule and tuning explicit so another discretization can be generated later.

This lets a program use cheap integers where they are valid without asserting that musical pitch is fundamentally integer-valued.

It also makes room for Duffin-style tuning values. A justly tuned, meantone, or otherwise unequal pitch can occupy the same continuous ambient space while a 12-TET rendering remains a particular projection or sampling.
