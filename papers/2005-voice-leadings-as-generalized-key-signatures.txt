Voice Leadings as Generalized Key Signatures: A Rational Reconstruction of Western Musical Notation
Dmitri Tymoczko
Music Theory Online 11.4 (2005)
DOI 10.30535/mto.11.4.1

Tymoczko treats a key signature as a collection of paths in pitch-class space. That turns ordinary notation into a device for describing voice leading from a basic scale to a target scale.

The paper proves several useful facts about minimal voice leading, voice crossing, and common-tone retention. In particular, it distinguishes different virtues a mapping may have rather than assuming there is always a single canonical mapping between two pitch collections.

Why it matters here

This paper gives a clean reason not to store only endpoints. Two notes can occupy the same pitch-class location while having arrived there by different paths. For music-theoretical purposes, those paths can matter.

A useful data object is therefore something like:

    voice-leading = source collection + target collection + per-voice paths

rather than merely:

    source set -> target set

That is especially relevant for enharmonic spelling. F-sharp and G-flat can coincide in equal-tempered pitch-class space while remaining different notational/path objects.
