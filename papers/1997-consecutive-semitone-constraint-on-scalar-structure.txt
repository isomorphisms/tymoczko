The Consecutive-Semitone Constraint on Scalar Structure: A Link Between Impressionism and Jazz
Dmitri Tymoczko
Intégral 11 (1997), pp. 135-179

This is the earliest Tymoczko publication I found in the line that eventually leads toward A Geometry of Music. It is not yet the Möbius-strip/orbifold paper. Its importance is that it treats scales as structured objects constrained by intervallic relations rather than as mere named collections.

The paper studies a 'consecutive-semitone constraint' on scale structure and uses it to connect harmonic/scalar practices in Impressionism and jazz. That concern with which scales permit which local motions is a precursor to Tymoczko's later treatment of scale space and voice-leading geometry.

For this repository, the useful idea is to treat a scale as an ordered or cyclic structure with constraints, not simply a bit-mask of twelve pitch classes. Different scale representations should be able to preserve adjacency, step size, spelling, and later a temperament-dependent physical realization.

Chronology note

This is 1997, not the 1980s. I found no Tymoczko publication from the 1980s. A likely neighboring memory is John Roeder's 1987 'A Geometric Representation of Pitch-Class Series,' which appears in later music-theory bibliographies around this geometric tradition.
