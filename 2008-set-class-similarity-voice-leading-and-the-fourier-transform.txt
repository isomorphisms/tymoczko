Set-Class Similarity, Voice Leading, and the Fourier Transform
Dmitri Tymoczko
Journal of Music Theory 52(2), 2008, pp. 251-272
DOI 10.1215/00222909-2009-017

What it does

Tymoczko compares two notions of distance between chord types: one based on minimal voice leading and another based on shared interval content expressed through Ian Quinn's Fourier-transform approach.

The paper shows that these are genuinely different constructions even though they can often give surprisingly similar results. Voice-leading distance is presented as the more flexible tool in many contexts.

Why it matters here

"Distance" between musical objects is not a single primitive number. Different metrics answer different questions.

Useful distinct metrics include:

- acoustical distance between sounding pitches;
- logarithmic/cents distance;
- voice-leading distance between chords;
- interval-content similarity;
- spelling or scale-path distance.

A system that reduces all of these to one float will eventually make incorrect equivalences. A float is useful as the value of a particular metric, but the metric itself needs an identity and provenance.

This is also a warning for comparing F-sharp and G-flat. Their acoustic distance can be zero in 12-TET while their spelling distance or tuning-relative distance is nonzero.
