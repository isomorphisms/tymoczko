The Geometry of Musical Chords
Dmitri Tymoczko
Science 313(5783), 2006, pp. 72-74
DOI 10.1126/science.1126287

This is the central early geometry paper.

Core construction

A chord is represented as a point in a geometrical space. A voice leading between two chords is represented by a path or line segment connecting their points. Short paths represent efficient voice leadings.

The spaces are quotient spaces: musical equivalences such as octave displacement and permutation of voices identify points that would otherwise be distinct. The resulting spaces are generally non-Euclidean orbifolds.

The Möbius strip

For two-note chords, imposing octave equivalence and ignoring the ordering of the two voices produces a space with the topology of a Möbius strip. This is the concrete source of the "Möbius strip of chords" idea associated with Tymoczko.

Higher-cardinality chords live in higher-dimensional analogues. The geometry exposes special regions corresponding to symmetric or nearly symmetric chords and helps explain why small voice leadings cluster around particular harmonic structures.

What the geometry forgets

A quotient space is useful because it deliberately identifies objects under chosen transformations. That also means it is dangerous to confuse the quotient with the original musical data.

If F-sharp and G-flat are assigned the same sounding pitch, a pure pitch-class geometry can place them at the same point. The spelling distinction must survive elsewhere if it matters. Likewise, a tuning model that gives F-sharp and G-flat different frequencies should feed distinct continuous coordinates into the geometry rather than forcing an equal-tempered collapse beforehand.

Implementation consequence

Keep the full musical object and derive quotient-space coordinates from it. Do not make the quotient-space representative the durable identity of the note or chord.

Useful separation:

    source musical object
        spelling
        tuning / ratio / frequency
        scale and harmonic context

    derived geometric object
        continuous pitch coordinates
        equivalence class
        orbifold coordinate
        voice-leading path / distance

The derived geometric representation can be recomputed under different equivalence relations without destroying the source information.
