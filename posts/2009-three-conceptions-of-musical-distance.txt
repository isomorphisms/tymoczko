Three Conceptions of Musical Distance
Dmitri Tymoczko
Mathematics and Computation in Music, 2009, pp. 258-272
DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-02394-1_24

What it does

This paper explicitly separates three different geometries that can all be described loosely as musical similarity or distance:

1. voice-leading distance, producing continuous quotient spaces or orbifolds;
2. acoustical distance, producing the Tonnetz and related tuning lattices;
3. interval-content distance, producing a higher-dimensional quality space associated with Ian Quinn's work.

The point is not merely that all three are useful. The point is that they answer different questions and should not be substituted for one another just because they sometimes produce similar-looking results.

This is the strongest direct bridge between the Tymoczko and Duffin sides of this repository.

Duffin's tuning problem belongs primarily to acoustical/tuning space. Tymoczko's chord-space problem belongs primarily to voice-leading space. A computational system should be able to map between them without pretending they are the same metric.

For example, an interval may have:

- an exact ratio, if supplied by a ratio-based tuning;
- a cents value, 1200 log2(ratio);
- a displacement in continuous pitch space;
- a voice-leading contribution inside a chord progression;
- a notational spelling and directed path.

These values are related but not interchangeable.

For F-sharp and G-flat, acoustic distance can be zero in 12-TET, nonzero in another tuning, while notational distinction remains present in either case.
