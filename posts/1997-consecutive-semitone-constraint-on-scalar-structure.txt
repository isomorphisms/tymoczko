The Consecutive-Semitone Constraint on Scalar Structure: A Link Between Impressionism and Jazz
Dmitri Tymoczko
Intégral 11 (1997), pp. 135-179

This is the earliest Tymoczko publication I found in the line that later leads toward A Geometry of Music. It is not yet the Möbius-strip/orbifold paper. The subject is scalar structure: which note collections satisfy a constraint on consecutive semitones, and how that helps connect harmonic practices in Impressionism and jazz.

Why it matters here

The important representation lesson is that a scale is not just a twelve-bit pitch-class set. Its adjacency relations and internal step structure matter. If two scales contain nearly the same pitch classes but organize them differently, that difference should remain visible.

This is already pointing toward the later voice-leading program: musical collections are structured objects connected by constrained motions, not just labels.

For an implementation, preserve at least:

- pitch spelling;
- cyclic scale order;
- step sizes;
- the sounding/log-frequency realization;
- the tuning or temperament that produced those sizes.

That makes it possible to keep an F-sharp and a G-flat distinct as symbolic/scale positions even if a later equal-tempered realization maps both to the same sounding pitch.

Chronology

This paper is from 1997. I found no Tymoczko papers from the 1980s. A likely source of the 1980s memory is John Roeder's 1987 article "A Geometric Representation of Pitch-Class Series," which belongs to the earlier geometrical music-theory literature that Tymoczko later engages.
